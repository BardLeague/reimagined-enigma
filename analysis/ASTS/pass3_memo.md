# ASTS — AST SpaceMobile — Investment Committee Memo (2026-07-07)

## 1. Thesis
AST SpaceMobile owns the best distribution asset in direct-to-smartphone satellite broadband — 50+ carrier partners covering ~3 billion subscribers, $1.2B contracted commitments, ~$3.5B cash, ~45 BlueBirds targeted in orbit by end of 2026. But FY2026 guided revenue is only $150–200M against a ~$33B market cap: the equity is a call option on flawless multi-year execution, currently priced as if the option were already in the money.

## 2. Why this opportunity exists
Expectations-gap verdict: **market too optimistic.** The market prices the bull case (full 243-satellite constellation, premium wholesale economics) as the central outcome; our base case gives it 25% weight and still reaches only $42 expected IV vs $85.13. The +31.2% week was sympathy momentum from the Rocket Lab–Iridium deal read-across, not an AST fundamental.

## 3. Key risks (top 3)
1. **Technological (High):** interference and handoff management across a 243-satellite fleet under commercial load is unproven engineering.
2. **Competitive (High):** Starlink direct-to-cell can compress wholesale economics before AST reaches scale.
3. **Bankruptcy/funding (Medium):** $191M quarterly net loss and 100+ satellites still to fund beyond the current $3.5B cash.

## 4. Key catalysts
- BlueBird 1–5 fully operational → first substantial AT&T/Verizon revenue (2026)
- Launch cadence toward ~45 satellites by end of 2026
- Q2 2026 earnings (Aug 17, 2026)
- Further carrier prepayments on the $1.2B committed base

## 7. Thesis breakers (verbatim)
- A launch failure destroying multiple Block 2 satellites
- FY2026 revenue guidance cut below $150M or BlueBird 1-5 operational slip into 2027
- A dilutive capital raise above ~15% of shares outstanding
- A major carrier partner (AT&T or Verizon) signing a competing direct-to-cell exclusive
- FCC or landing-rights setback in a top-5 market
