# PEP — PepsiCo — Investment Committee Memo (2026-07-08)

## 1. Thesis
PepsiCo looks like the safe one — Q1 beat, volumes stabilizing, Elliott driving a turnaround, 4% yield, beta 0.31. The arithmetic disagrees: the dividend consumed 105% of earnings and 111% of free cash flow last year on a ~2.45x levered balance sheet, while GLP-1-era consumption structurally pressures the snack core. At $145 (22.5x) the market pays a safety premium for safety that is currently an accounting appearance. Expected IV: $79.20.

## 2. Why this opportunity exists
Expectations-gap verdict: **market too optimistic.** This is the classic staples value trap: a fat yield anchoring a multiple the cash flows no longer support. Our base case gives the Elliott plan real credit (~40% FCF growth by 2030) and still lands ~45% below the quote. Q2 earnings print tomorrow (July 9) with analysts cutting targets into it.

## 3. Key risks (top 3)
1. **Value trap (High)** — the yield is not a margin of safety when it is debt-financed. (This rating excludes PEP from our ranked list by rule.)
2. **Dividend arithmetic (primary model risk):** stagnant FCF forces either debt-funded dividends (compounding leverage) or a cut (cracking the shareholder base).
3. **Structural demand:** GLP-1 consumption shifts are secular, not cyclical, for salty snacks.

## 4. Key catalysts
- Q2 2026 earnings, July 9, 2026 (tomorrow)
- Elliott turnaround milestones (margins, portfolio moves)
- Dividend-coverage trajectory (FCF vs the $7.9B payout)
